**Relation to the two PDF-check sheets (AI checks, not a human verification; the status stays UNVERIFIED)**

Two sibling sheets written the same day compare the AI-DRAFT blocks with the PDFs the user attached, page by page: `jhr/evidence/unverified_fearn1990.md` (FE) and `jhr/evidence/unverified_cherdron1978.md` (CH); their line numbers are those of 2026-11-22 14:05. This sheet follows the AI-DRAFT blocks, as instructed, and narrows the wording wherever those checks found that the draft says more than the paper. An independent look at the text layer of both PDFs for this sheet (Fearn p.601-604, Cherdron p.20-22 and p.25) agreed with their verdicts (audit: Cherdron p.22 is a full-page figure without text; the passages are on p.20, p.21 and p.25). Points that change how the cards below may be worded:

- Fearn et al. (1990). The paper's term is symmetry-breaking bifurcation; the word pitchfork, the term imperfect bifurcation and the word slowly are not in the paper, and hysteresis is never applied to the authors' own results (FE L7, L58). The value 40.45 is the computed value for a perfectly symmetric channel; the experiment showed no critical Reynolds number (FE L18). The paper gives no rule that the sign of an imperfection decides the side (FE L25, L63). Agreement with the experiment is called satisfactory away from the immediate vicinity of the bifurcation point (PDF p.601; FE L18), which is narrower than "実験とよく一致" (RM L1188).
- Cherdron et al. (1978). The Reynolds number is defined on p.20, not p.18 (CH L20; PDF p.20). The figure-read threshold "約 125" for expansion ratio 2 and aspect ratio 8 is wrong according to the check (CH L24). Of the two imperfection tests, one is geometric and one is a flow imperfection, and their size is not given (CH L26, L40). The way of forcing the flow to the other side is not described in this paper, which refers to Durst et al. (1974) (CH L30; PDF p.21). The paper does not use the word bifurcation (CH L36, L100) and does not call its flow regime transitional (CH L35); it does speak of the transition from symmetric to asymmetric flow (PDF p.20). It is not the first report of asymmetric flow in a symmetric expansion (CH L41; PDF p.13).
