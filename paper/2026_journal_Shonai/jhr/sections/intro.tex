\begin{en}[I-1]
A reservoir of a pumped-storage hydropower (PSH) plant receives water in one operating mode and releases it in the other, and the entering jet can be a main source of mixing. As of 2012, PSH accounted for more than 99\% of the bulk electricity storage capacity installed worldwide \citep{rehmanPumpedHydroEnergy2015} \PHref{recent PSH statistics}. Plants with an hourly storage cycle can be operated in short-circuit mode and allow more than 100 reversals per day \citep{huntExistingNewArrangements2020} \PHref{primary source (Goekler and Meusburger, cited by Hunt et al.)}, and changes of operating mode are expected to become more frequent as variable renewable generation expands \PHref{source stating that changes of operating mode become more frequent as variable renewable generation expands}. In Lake Grimsel, the lower reservoir of the Grimsel~2 plant, discharging into the lake was estimated to introduce about 25 times the turbulent kinetic energy introduced by wind \citep{mullerFlowFieldReservoir2018}. In a laboratory basin with a reversible port, the suspended sediment ratio \PH{definition of the suspended sediment ratio} was higher during sequences of flow into the basin than out of it, which was attributed mainly to mixing by the kinetic energy of the entering jet \citep{mullerExperimentsEffectInflow2017}. The flow set up by such a jet therefore matters in practice, particularly where sedimentation of upper reservoirs is an operational concern \PHref{source stating that sedimentation of the upper reservoirs of PSH plants is an operational concern}.
\end{en}

\begin{en}[I-2]
In shallow rectangular reservoirs with through-flow, the pattern taken by the entering jet has been classified mainly by geometry. From dye visualization, \citet{dufresneClassificationFlowPatterns2010} distinguished a symmetric pattern without reattachment from asymmetric patterns with one or two reattachment points, and expressed the transition between them by a purely geometric shape parameter. Velocity fields measured in an independent facility at fixed depth and discharge were likewise organized by a shape parameter, with bistable or unstable behaviour in the transition range \citep{camnasioExperimentalStudyVelocity2011}. The deflection of the jet towards one side wall has been explained by the Coanda effect \citep{dewalsExperimentalNumericalAnalysis2008,camnasioExperimentalStudyVelocity2011}. Where the Froude number or the discharge was varied, the reported effects were a shift of the first reattachment length \citep{dufresneClassificationFlowPatterns2010}, the occurrence of meandering, decided together with the shape parameter \citep{peltierExperimentalInvestigationMeandering2014}, and, with a pipe inlet, unstable flow in a long reservoir at low Froude number \citep{chagdaliFlowPatternsShallow2025}. The pattern matters for sediment, since the trapping efficiency measured with plastic granules rose sharply at the transition from the symmetric pattern to the asymmetric pattern with one reattachment point \citep{dufresneExperimentalInvestigationFlow2010}.
\end{en}

\begin{en}[I-3]
A jet that deflects in a symmetric reservoir takes one of two mirror-image states, or branches, and in one such reservoir a slight change of the initial conditions produced the mirror image of the deflected pattern \citep{kantoushFlowFieldInvestigation2008}. In reservoirs whose geometry, inlet and outlet were all symmetric, \citet{dewalsExperimentalNumericalAnalysis2008} attributed the asymmetric flow found in four of eight geometries to the growth of small perturbations towards one of two mirror-image stable solutions. They described the transition by a linear stability analysis and a geometric parameter. For low-Reynolds-number flow in ducts with a plane symmetric sudden expansion, \citet{cherdronAsymmetricFlowsInstabilities1978}\unverified{} reported that an imposed imperfection of the geometry or of the approach flow fixed the side of the shorter recirculation region. In test sections built with particular care, that region could persist on either side \citep{cherdronAsymmetricFlowsInstabilities1978}\unverified{}. \citet{fearnNonlinearFlowPhenomena1990}\unverified{} reported that the asymmetry there arises at a symmetry-breaking bifurcation which small imperfections of the apparatus disconnect, so that a continuous increase in Reynolds number always led to the same branch. After an impulsive start at a Reynolds number above 50, in contrast, the larger separation region attached to either wall without preference \citep{fearnNonlinearFlowPhenomena1990}\unverified{}. For a channel with a sudden expansion followed by a contraction, \citet{mullinFlowSymmetricChannel2003} describe the bifurcation as a pitchfork and state that small geometric imperfections strongly disconnect it.
\end{en}

\begin{en}[I-4]
The intake/outlet structure of a PSH reservoir carries the flow in both directions \citep{zhangParameterAnalysisPerformance2020,bermudezHydraulicModelStudy2017,mullerFlowFieldReservoir2018}, and studies of deep water bodies indicate that discharging into a reservoir and withdrawing from it act on the reservoir very differently. In Lake Grimsel, which is up to 100~m deep, withdrawal affected only the immediate vicinity of the structure, whereas discharging into the lake formed a backflow zone and a large circulation cell that kept rotating after discharging stopped \citep{mullerFlowFieldReservoir2018}. In the deep laboratory basin of \citet{mullerInfluenceOutflowSequences2012}, with a water depth of about 0.58 times its width, discharging into the basin through a reversible port produced a jet with two horizontal recirculation cells and one vertical cell. During withdrawal, only the flow near the port was directed towards it, and the measured kinetic energy fell to 5--10\% of the peak reached while discharging. The cells often persisted into the following withdrawal, so that the flow depended on its history. A deflection of the jet at two of three port elevations was attributed to the geometric asymmetry caused by an overflow section \citep{mullerInfluenceOutflowSequences2012}.
\end{en}

\begin{en}[I-5]
To our knowledge, it has not been established whether the discharge influences which mirror-image state the flow takes in a shallow basin of fixed geometry. First, in the shallow-reservoir studies cited above, the principal control parameter is geometric and several used a single discharge \citep{dewalsExperimentalNumericalAnalysis2008,camnasioExperimentalStudyVelocity2011,kantoushFlowFieldInvestigation2008}. Where the Froude number or the discharge was varied, the reported effects concern the reattachment length and the meandering or steadiness of the flow \citep{dufresneClassificationFlowPatterns2010,peltierExperimentalInvestigationMeandering2014,chagdaliFlowPatternsShallow2025}. Second, the studies cited above in which the Reynolds number is the control parameter concern confined flows at low Reynolds number \citep{cherdronAsymmetricFlowsInstabilities1978,fearnNonlinearFlowPhenomena1990}\unverified{} \citep{mullinFlowSymmetricChannel2003}, whereas for shallow wakes viscosity is immaterial above a Reynolds number of about 1000 \PH{definition of this Reynolds number for shallow wakes} and a bed-friction parameter alone governs the flow \citep{chenAbsoluteConvectiveInstabilities1997}. Third, for a fixed geometry, we are not aware of any experiment in which the discharge alone was varied, the runs were replicated and the side taken was counted, nor of any report that the preferred side reverses as the discharge or the Reynolds number changes. Fourth, the only laboratory studies known to us of a rectangular basin with a single reversible port varied the discharge but used a deep basin and point measurements and addressed sediment \citep{mullerInfluenceOutflowSequences2012,mullerExperimentsEffectInflow2017}; to our knowledge, a shallow basin of this kind has not been examined.

\begin{en}[I-6]
This study tests, in a shallow rectangular basin with a single reversible port at mid-width of one short wall, whether the discharge selects the branch of the basin-scale circulation. Water was either discharged into the basin through the port (inflow) or withdrawn through it (outflow), corresponding to the pumping and generating modes of an upper reservoir. Three discharge levels were each run four times in each mode, with the inflow runs arranged in a randomized complete block design with days as blocks. Surface particle image velocimetry (PIV) recorded the velocity field as the water level passed the top of the port. We ask, first and principally, whether the discharge governs which branch is selected and how firmly it is held; second, how the surface flow differs between inflow and outflow; and third, what role the relative submergence $\ha$ plays. Here $\hdep$ is the water depth and $\aport$ the port height. The study is limited to the flow, without sediment, and to velocities at the water surface. The work is a prototype-informed laboratory experiment, not a strict similitude model, and its data are also intended for validating numerical models.
\end{en}
